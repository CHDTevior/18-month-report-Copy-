\chapter{Summary of Work}

\section{Research narrative}

This thesis builds a text-to-motion system that is controllable at the
granularity practitioners work with --- a named joint, a sketched path, a
specific interval --- and general enough to carry over to a new body or a new
task. The work reaches this through one representational commitment: motion is
represented as \emph{addressable tokens}, per body part and then per joint of
any skeleton, and control is expressed as an operation on those tokens rather
than as a stronger condition on a generator.

The first work, MCRE~\cite{sun2024mcre}, builds a shared conditioning space for
text and motion. A lightweight motion adapter aligned to a frozen text
encoder~\cite{radford2021learning} places captions and motions in one space, so
that semantic edits correspond to linear directions in that space.

The second work makes generation fast and unbounded. Diffusion text-to-motion
models~\cite{tevet2022human,chen2023executing,zhang2024motiondiffuse} sample
iteratively and train at a fixed horizon, which rules out interactive use.
Hi-RQCT~\cite{sun2025hirqct} and then MOGO~\cite{sun2025mogo} (AAAI 2026) discretize motion
with a scale-adaptive residual vector
quantizer~\cite{vandenoord2017neural,zeghidour2022soundstream} and decode every
quantization level in a single causal forward pass, giving real-time synthesis,
unbounded sequence length and mid-stream prompt switching. This establishes the
discrete substrate on which Chapters~4 and 5 build.

The third work adds precise spatial control. Placing one hand at one point, or a
root along a sketched path, calls for tokens that name individual body parts. This
work treats an anatomy-aligned part codebook as a control surface and uses that
one representation twice: as the sites where control enters, and as
the geometry in which the correction is measured. \textbf{PartVQ} learns one
codebook per anatomical part ($Q=6$, with the partition derived from data), so a
token names both a moment in time and a part of the body. \textbf{T-Concat}
unpacks the part codebooks along the sequence axis, giving every (frame, part)
pair its own attention slot. \textbf{KV-Control} feeds a
target trajectory into a frozen masked transformer as extra key/value memory at
every self-attention layer, training 10.5M parameters and leaving the query
stream, the text cross-attention and every backbone weight untouched. The same
codebooks record which changes the decoder was trained to absorb, so they define
a geometry in which a correction can be measured: \textbf{CodeGeo} moves the
generated clip onto the commanded path by the smallest change that geometry
allows, in at most eight analytic steps in place of MaskControl's
1,600-iteration refinement~\cite{pinyoanuntapong2025maskcontrol}, with a step
metric whose anisotropy must match how far the anchors spread across the body.
The result that matters most for the thesis is an ablation: the same control
interface on a channel-stacked trunk of matched quality tracks with 2.2 times
the error. Control accuracy is a property of the representation, not of the
injection alone.

Because control accuracy comes from the representation, the fourth work carries
that representation beyond a single body. HumanML3D~\cite{guo2022generating},
KIT-ML~\cite{plappert2016kitkit} and AMASS~\cite{mahmood2019amass} share one
SMPL~\cite{loper2015smpl} skeleton. Games, film and virtual production
include many characters that are not human, and a studio has hundreds of rigs,
each with its own joint count, naming and helper bones. TopX carries the
addressable-token idea from one body to any body. Its token is one joint at one frame rather than one part at one frame.
\textbf{TopX-17} converts any rigged animation from its kinematic tree, rest pose
and motion alone, and standardizes every (rig, joint, channel) cell by its own
statistics, so that rigs of very different proportions share one network input
with no template, retargeting or inverse kinematics. The skeleton enters as a
prompt --- the rig's rest pose leads the sequence as a clean frame and tree
distances bias spatial attention --- so nothing in the network is indexed by rig.
This is the same principle as KV-Control, applied to a different condition: in
both, what the model must obey is made addressable inside attention rather than
baked into the architecture. One 0.3B-parameter flow-matching transformer serves
311 animal rigs of 34 to 102 joints, and an unseen rig is served by the same
forward pass.

Two directions follow. The first extends TopX from its animal library to
arbitrary skeletons --- humans, animals and fantasy creatures --- including
skeletons never seen in training. The second extends generation from one
instruction to ordered, composite requests. Both are set out in Chapter~3.

\section{Completed contributions}

\subsection{Shared conditional representation for text and motion}

Text-to-motion models must reconcile modalities that differ in dimensionality,
length and structure. Conditioning a generator on an embedding from an encoder
that has never seen motion couples the two representations loosely, which makes
editing difficult to express.

This work trains a lightweight motion adapter aligned to a frozen pretrained
text encoder, mapping a motion and its caption to nearby points in one
conditional space. In this shared space, semantic edits correspond to linear
directions, so a motion is edited by moving its representation rather than by
re-prompting the generator.

Published at the ECCV 2024 Workshop on Foundation Models for 3D
Humans~\cite{sun2024mcre}.

\subsection{Hierarchical one-pass autoregressive generation}

Tokenization-based motion models~\cite{chuan2022tm2t,guo2024momask} decode
faster than diffusion, but residual quantization schemes generally require one
decoding pass per quantization level, and error accumulates over long
autoregressive rollouts.

This work introduces two components. MoSA-VQ is a residual vector quantizer
with learnable per-level scaling and a cross-level decorrelation objective; the
decorrelation term minimizes the batch-wise covariance between a quantized
vector and the residual passed to the next level, encouraging
statistical orthogonality across the hierarchy so each level encodes what the
previous levels did not. This improves codebook utilization and yields more
compact codes under a fixed budget. The RQHC-Transformer is a causal decoder
whose structure mirrors the quantization hierarchy and which emits all levels in
one forward pass, removing per-level decoding cost while preserving temporal
coherence.

Together these give real-time decoding, generation of unbounded length,
and injection of a new text prompt at an arbitrary point in the sequence --- the
property that makes the system usable for avatar control and teleoperation
rather than offline clip synthesis. A textual condition alignment module
normalizes free-form input into structured instructions before generation,
improving robustness on prompts unlike those seen in training.

Removing the decorrelation objective degrades generation FID and retrieval
accuracy across all metrics.

The decoder was published at CVMP 2025 as Hi-RQCT~\cite{sun2025hirqct}; the full
system was published at AAAI 2026 as MOGO~\cite{sun2025mogo}.

\subsection{Part-addressable control and codebook-geometry correction}

A text prompt gives the user no handle on placement: the generator alone decides
where the body goes, and no amount of prompt editing pins a clip to a root path,
a foot contact or a planner's trajectory. Existing trajectory controllers pay for
this control in one of two ways. ControlNet-style adapters such as
OmniControl~\cite{xie2024omnicontrol} and
MaskControl~\cite{pinyoanuntapong2025maskcontrol} wire a near-duplicate backbone
branch into the trunk, and several also rely on inference-time optimization ---
MaskControl on a 1,600-iteration refinement. Test-time optimization methods add
no branch but perturb latents or noise until the targets are met, moving each
sample away from the base model's distribution. Both costs have the same origin:
the control signal, the motion representation and the attention pathway are not
aligned.

This work treats the anatomy-aligned part codebook as a control surface and uses
it twice.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{PartVQ} learns $Q=6$ per-part codebooks with a data-driven
  partition, so each token names a part at a frame.
\item \textbf{T-Concat} unpacks the part codebooks along the sequence axis into
  $49\times 6 = 294$ (frame, part) attention slots on a frozen 20-layer
  backbone, preserving per-position text cross-attention.
\item \textbf{KV-Control} appends control-conditioned key/value entries at every
  self-attention layer through a near-identity low-rank branch. The trainable
  budget is 10.5M parameters (a shared 9M trajectory encoder and 1.5M of
  per-layer injection weights); the query stream and all backbone weights are
  frozen.
\item \textbf{CodeGeo} refines the generated motion analytically. The
  trajectory becomes $A$ anchor constraints, and each step solves a
  $3A\times 3A$ damped least-squares
  problem $(\mathbf{J}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{J}^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{r}$
  under a training-free metric $\mathbf{M}=\Sigma(\rho)$ taken from the
  codebook's own second-order statistics. At most eight steps replace
  MaskControl's 1,600-iteration refinement.
\end{itemize}

The work also establishes how the metric must be set. Its anisotropy has to
match how far the anchor set spreads across body parts: the setting best for
single-joint control is ruled out for multi-joint control. On pelvis
control CodeGeo tracks to 0.24\,cm at FID 0.067 in 1/21 of the per-sample time of
the refinement it replaces; on multi-joint control it reaches 0.25\,cm at FID
0.072, improving on MaskControl's 0.72\,cm and 0.083 on both axes. The same
interface on a channel-stacked trunk of matched quality tracks with 2.2 times the
error, showing that the accuracy comes from the representation, and the
interface transfers to an RVQ-based MoMask backbone~\cite{guo2024momask}.

This work is under review at ICLR 2027.

\subsection{Skeleton-aware text-to-motion across heterogeneous topologies}

Text-to-motion generation has been built for one body. The joint count, joint
order and pose parameterization of a single skeleton are baked into the input
projection and into any codebook, so every new character means a new model or a
retargeting step~\cite{aberman2020skeletonaware}, which degrades as morphology
diverges. Serving hundreds of production rigs with one network meets four
obstacles: the rigs share no common tensor; the network must be told what each
joint of an unfamiliar tree is without a parameter per skeleton; a loss summed
over bodies of 34 to 102 joints lets the largest dominate; and a rig outside the
training library must be served with no clips or a handful.

TopX is a text-conditioned motion diffusion transformer built on three design
choices that answer these obstacles.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{TopX-17} is a per-joint representation of 17 channels (position,
  6D rotation delta from rest, velocity, contact and root trajectory). Any rigged
  animation is converted from its kinematic tree, rest pose and motion alone, and
  every (rig, joint, channel) cell is standardized by its own statistics. This
  per-cell normalization is worth 1.7--2.0 points of R@1 over a per-rig scale.
\item \textbf{In-context skeleton conditioning.} One token per joint per frame;
  the rig's clean rest pose leads the sequence, joint descriptions are embedded
  with LLM2Vec, and a tree-distance bias with learned ancestor tables lets
  spatial attention tell parent from child. The flat padded-joint construction by
  which fixed-skeleton generators are usually extended trails this design by
  0.19--0.20 of R@1 and multiplies FID by 3.7.
\item \textbf{Grouped flow matching} with calibrated channel weights keeps every
  channel group's share of the gradient independent of the number of cells it
  spans; the calibration is worth 0.036--0.043 of R@1 and makes a 303M-parameter
  model train stably.
\end{itemize}

The training corpus is 311 animal rigs with 34 to 102 deformation joints across
108 distinct kinematic trees, carrying 77,894 captioned clips (6.2M frames) from
the AniMo4D annotation set~\cite{wang2025animo}. Scored by a skeleton-aware
text--motion evaluator, TopX reaches R@1 0.867 against 0.904 for real clips, with
FID 0.005, and retrieval and FID improve monotonically from 36M to 303M
parameters. The output is what a rig plays directly: per-joint rotations and a
world-space root trajectory, with no inverse-kinematics fit.

Rigs outside the library are served by the same forward pass. On four held-out
Truebones rigs (buffalo, gazelle, dragon and spider, the whole Truebones zoo being
kept out of training), a low-rank adapter trained on 8--26 clips brings the
FK--pose gap to 0.23--0.35 bone lengths, with the largest improvement on the two
rigs farthest from the library.

This work is under review at ICLR 2027.

\subsection{Collaborative work}

Three further motion systems were developed with colleagues in the group.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{MotionDuet}~\cite{zhang2026motionduet} (CVPR 2026) aligns
  text-conditioned motion generation with the distribution of video-derived
  representations through dual text--video conditioning. I was responsible for
  bridging the video feature extractor into the model and for the training
  design.
\item \textbf{MoGeFlow}~\cite{fang2026mogeflow} generates text-conditioned motion
  as a continuous flow through motion-codebook geometry rather than by
  categorical code prediction. I contributed to the design of the DiT backbone
  and to the ablations on the role of the codebook at inference.
\item \textbf{UMO}~\cite{zhan2026umo} models sparse motion in a unified framework
  for real-time co-speech avatars. I was responsible for the data format, the
  motion-control training and the design of the motion-editing training
  framework.
\end{itemize}

\section{Generalization to arbitrary skeletons}

\workitem{%
TopX takes the skeleton as input and is trained on a library of animal rigs.
This work extends it to arbitrary skeletons --- humans, animals and fantasy
creatures, with different joint counts, joint naming and rest-pose conventions
--- and completes Chapter~6.}{%
Given a skeleton (kinematic tree, rest pose and joint names) and a text
description, to generate per-joint rotations and a root trajectory that play
directly on the rig, for skeletons never seen in training, without per-rig
statistics or external retargeting, and fast enough for real-time use in games.}{%
Everything the model needs to know about a rig can be read from the skeleton
itself. When rig-specific quantities are derived from the kinematic tree, rest
pose and joint names rather than from the rig's own motion, one model
generalizes to unseen skeletons, and the same text is realized as the motion
appropriate to each body.}{%
\begin{enumerate}
\item Extend training from the animal library to heterogeneous skeletons of
  humans, animals and fantasy creatures.
\item Derive all rig-specific quantities from the skeleton, so that an unseen
  rig needs no motion data and no external retargeting.
\item Evaluate on held-out skeletons, including body-plan families absent from
  training, and on text following across families.
\item Reach real-time inference for in-game use.
\end{enumerate}}
