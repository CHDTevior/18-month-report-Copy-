\chapter{Summary of Work}

\section{Research narrative}

This thesis builds a text-to-motion system that is controllable at the level
practitioners actually work at --- a named joint, a sketched path, a specific
interval --- and general enough to survive a change of body or a change of
task. The work reaches this through a single technical commitment: motion is
represented as a \emph{part-tokenized substrate}, and control is expressed as
an operation on that substrate rather than as a stronger condition on a
generator.

The first work, MCRE~\cite{sun2024mcre}, built the conditioning interface. A
lightweight motion adapter trained against a frozen text
encoder~\cite{radford2021learning} places captions and motions in one space, so
that semantic edits become displacement vectors and conditions compose by
addition. This is the interface every later chapter inherits. It also fixes the
granularity of what it can express: one vector per clip, which says what a
motion is but not where or when any part of it happens.

The second work made generation fast and unbounded. Diffusion text-to-motion
models~\cite{tevet2022human,chen2023executing,zhang2024motiondiffuse} sample
iteratively and train at a fixed horizon, which rules out interactive use.
Hi-RQCT~\cite{sun2025hirqct} and then MOGO~\cite{sun2025mogo} discretise motion
with a scale-adaptive residual vector
quantizer~\cite{vandenoord2017neural,zeghidour2022soundstream} and decode every
quantization level in a single causal forward pass, giving real-time synthesis,
unbounded sequence length and mid-stream prompt switching. MOGO was published at
AAAI 2026. This established the discrete substrate the thesis works on, and
sharpened the control problem: a token grid is fast, but a flat grid of
whole-body codes offers nothing to address when a user wants one hand in one
place.

The third work is the centre of the thesis, and it answers that problem with
one representation used twice. \textbf{PartVQ} learns one codebook per
anatomical part ($Q=6$, with the partition derived from data), so a token names
both a moment in time and a part of the body. \textbf{T-Concat} unpacks the part
codebooks along the sequence axis, giving every (frame, part) pair its own
attention slot. \textbf{KV-Control}~\cite{sun2026codegeo} then feeds a target
trajectory into a frozen masked transformer as extra key/value memory at every
self-attention layer: 10.5M trainable parameters, with the query stream, the text
cross-attention and every backbone weight untouched. The same codebooks also
record which changes the decoder was trained to absorb, so they define a
geometry in which a correction can be measured. \textbf{CodeGeo} moves the
generated clip onto the commanded path by the smallest change that geometry
allows, in at most eight damped analytic steps in place of the 1,600-iteration
refinement used by prior controllers. The anisotropy of that step metric is not
a free parameter: it must match how far the commanded anchors spread across the
body, and joints confined to one part and joints spread over the whole body
prefer opposite settings. On pelvis control the method tracks to 0.24\,cm at FID
0.067 in 1/21 of the per-sample time; on multi-joint control it reaches 0.25\,cm
at FID 0.072, against MaskControl's 0.72\,cm and 0.083~\cite{pinyoanuntapong2025maskcontrol}.
The result that matters most for the thesis is an ablation: the same control
interface on a channel-stacked trunk of matched quality tracks with 2.2 times
the error. Control accuracy is a property of the representation, not of the
injection alone.

PartVQ has since become the substrate other work in the group builds on. In
MoGeFlow~\cite{fang2026mogeflow} the part codebooks are shown to carry
measurable, decoder-causal kinematic geometry, which licenses generation as a
continuous flow through codebook space rather than categorical index prediction;
in AnchorRoute~\cite{fang2026anchorroute} the same addressable tokens carry
sparse anchors both as generation-time conditioning and as residuals that route
interval-local refinement.

That ablation also exposes the substrate's boundary. PartVQ's partition is
learned, but the body it partitions is fixed: HumanML3D~\cite{guo2022generating},
KIT-ML~\cite{plappert2016kitkit} and AMASS~\cite{mahmood2019amass} share one
SMPL~\cite{loper2015smpl} skeleton, and every codebook is learned for that
morphology. Games, film and virtual production are populated by characters that
are not human, and a studio has hundreds of rigs, each with its own joint count,
naming and helper bones. The fourth work, TopX~\cite{sun2026topx}, carries the
addressable-token idea from one body to any body. Its token is one joint at one
frame rather than one part at one frame; \textbf{TopX-17} converts any rigged
animation from its kinematic tree, rest pose and motion alone, and standardises
every (rig, joint, channel) cell by its own statistics, so that rigs of very
different proportions share one network input with no template, retargeting or
inverse kinematics. The skeleton enters as a prompt --- the rig's rest pose
leads the sequence as a clean frame and tree distances bias spatial attention
--- so nothing in the network is indexed by rig. This is the same principle as
KV-Control, applied to a different condition: in both, what the model must obey
is made addressable inside attention rather than baked into the architecture.
One 0.3B-parameter flow-matching transformer serves 311 animal rigs of 34 to 102
joints, reaching R@1 0.867 against 0.904 for real clips and FID 0.005, and an
unseen rig is served by the same forward pass.

Two limits remain. First, TopX serves rigs outside its library zero-shot, but
bodies far from it --- a winged dragon, a many-legged spider --- still need a
per-rig adapter trained on roughly ten clips, and the per-cell statistics that
make a rig usable are themselves computed from that rig's motion. Serving a new
body plan with no clips at all is the out-of-distribution problem the TopX line
has not yet closed. Second, every model above executes one instruction. Real
requests are ordered and composite, and concatenating independently generated
clips leaves seams and drifting style~\cite{shafir2024priormdm,barquero2024flowmdm}.
These two limits define the remaining period and are set out in Chapter~3.

\section{Completed contributions}

\subsection{Shared conditional representation for text and motion}

Text-to-motion models must reconcile modalities that differ in dimensionality,
length and structure. The standard approach conditions a generator on an
embedding from an encoder that has never seen motion, which couples the two
representations loosely and makes anything other than one-shot generation ---
editing, interpolation, composition --- awkward to express.

This work trained a lightweight motion adapter against a frozen pre-trained
text encoder, mapping a motion and its caption to nearby points in one
conditional space. Because the space is shared and the objective is
contrastive, semantic relationships between captions transfer to motions:
changing the speed, direction or affect of a movement is a displacement in the
conditional space rather than a new prompt, and several conditions combine by
composition in the same space.

The result is the conditioning interface reused by every subsequent chapter,
and a precise statement of what clip-level conditioning cannot do. That
statement sets the agenda for Chapters~4 and 5.

Published at the ECCV 2024 Workshop on Foundation Models for 3D
Humans~\cite{sun2024mcre}.

\subsection{Hierarchical one-pass autoregressive generation}

Tokenization-based motion models~\cite{chuan2022tm2t,guo2024momask} decode
faster than diffusion, but residual quantization schemes generally require one
decoding pass per quantization level, and error accumulates over long
autoregressive rollouts.

This work contributed two components. MoSA-VQ is a residual vector quantizer
with learnable per-level scaling and a cross-level decorrelation objective; the
decorrelation term minimises the batch-wise covariance between a quantized
vector and the residual passed to the next level, enforcing approximate
statistical orthogonality across the hierarchy so each level encodes what the
previous levels did not. This improves codebook utilisation and yields more
compact codes under a fixed budget. The RQHC-Transformer is a causal decoder
whose structure mirrors the quantization hierarchy and which emits all levels in
one forward pass, removing per-level decoding cost while preserving temporal
coherence.

Together these give near real-time decoding, generation of unbounded length,
and injection of a new text prompt at an arbitrary point in the sequence --- the
property that makes the system usable for avatar control and teleoperation
rather than offline clip synthesis. A textual condition alignment module
normalises free-form input into structured instructions before generation,
improving robustness on prompts unlike those seen in training.

Ablations confirmed both components. Removing the decorrelation objective
degraded generation FID and retrieval accuracy consistently across metrics.
Varying codebook capacity showed that the configuration minimising
reconstruction error is not the one maximising generation quality, which
established that quantization error here is structured rather than uniform
noise --- the observation that motivated moving to an anatomy-aligned
decomposition in the next work.

The decoder was published at CVMP 2025 as Hi-RQCT~\cite{sun2025hirqct}; the full
system was published at AAAI 2026 as MOGO~\cite{sun2025mogo}.

\subsection{Part-addressable control and codebook-geometry correction}

A text prompt gives the user no handle on placement: the generator alone decides
where the body goes, and no amount of prompt editing pins a clip to a root path,
a foot contact or a planner's trajectory. Learned trajectory controllers pay for
this control in one of two ways. ControlNet-style adapters such as
OmniControl~\cite{xie2024omnicontrol} and
MaskControl~\cite{pinyoanuntapong2025maskcontrol} wire a near-duplicate backbone
branch into the trunk, and several still rely on inference-time optimisation ---
MaskControl on a 1,600-iteration refinement. Test-time optimisation methods add
no branch but perturb latents or noise until the targets are met, moving each
sample away from the base model's distribution. Both costs have the same origin:
the control signal, the motion representation and the attention pathway are not
aligned.

This work treats the anatomy-aligned part codebook as a control surface and uses
it twice.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{PartVQ} learns $Q=6$ per-part codebooks with a data-driven
  partition, so each token names a part at a frame.
\item \textbf{T-Concat} unpacks the part codebooks along the sequence axis into
  $49\times 6 = 294$ (frame, part) attention slots on a frozen 20-layer
  backbone, preserving per-position text cross-attention.
\item \textbf{KV-Control} appends control-conditioned key/value entries at every
  self-attention layer through a near-identity low-rank branch. The trainable
  budget is 10.5M parameters (a shared 9M trajectory encoder and 1.5M of
  per-layer injection weights); the query stream and all backbone weights are
  frozen.
\item \textbf{CodeGeo} finishes the control analytically. The trajectory becomes
  anchor constraints, and each step solves a $3A\times 3A$ damped least-squares
  problem $(\mathbf{J}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{J}^{\top}+\lambda\mathbf{I})\,\mathbf{y}=\mathbf{r}$
  under a training-free metric $\mathbf{M}=\Sigma(\rho)$ taken from the
  codebook's own second-order statistics. At most eight steps replace the
  inherited 1,600-iteration refinement.
\end{itemize}

The work also establishes how the metric must be set. Its anisotropy has to
match how far the anchor set spreads across body parts, because a scalar trust
region can serve only rows the metric prices comparably: the value best for
single-joint control is the one value the multi-joint sweep rules out, and the
two tasks move in opposite directions in FID as anisotropy weakens. On pelvis
control CodeGeo tracks to 0.24\,cm at FID 0.067 in 1/21 of the per-sample time of
the refinement it replaces; on multi-joint control it reaches 0.25\,cm at FID
0.072, improving on MaskControl's 0.72\,cm and 0.083 on both axes. The same
interface on a channel-stacked trunk of matched quality tracks with 2.2 times the
error, and the interface transfers to an RVQ and MoMask
backbone~\cite{guo2024momask}.

This work is under review at AAAI 2027~\cite{sun2026codegeo}.

\subsection{Skeleton-aware text-to-motion across heterogeneous topologies}

Text-to-motion generation has been built for one body. The joint count, joint
order and pose parameterisation of a single skeleton are baked into the input
projection and into any codebook, so every new character means a new model or a
retargeting step~\cite{aberman2020skeletonaware}, which degrades as morphology
diverges. Serving hundreds of production rigs with one network meets four
obstacles: the rigs share no common tensor; the network must be told what each
joint of an unfamiliar tree is without a parameter per skeleton; a loss summed
over bodies of 34 to 102 joints lets the largest dominate; and a rig outside the
training library must be served with no clips or a handful.

TopX is a text-conditioned motion diffusion transformer built on three design
choices that answer these obstacles.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{TopX-17} is a per-joint representation of 17 channels (position,
  6D rotation delta from rest, velocity, contact and root trajectory). Any rigged
  animation is converted from its kinematic tree, rest pose and motion alone, and
  every (rig, joint, channel) cell is standardised by its own statistics. This
  per-cell normalisation is worth 1.7--2.0 points of R@1 over a per-rig scale.
\item \textbf{In-context skeleton conditioning.} One token per joint per frame;
  the rig's clean rest pose leads the sequence, joint descriptions are embedded
  with LLM2Vec, and a tree-distance bias with learned ancestor tables lets
  spatial attention tell parent from child. The flat padded-joint construction by
  which fixed-skeleton generators are usually extended trails this design by
  0.19--0.20 of R@1 and triples FID.
\item \textbf{Grouped flow matching} with calibrated channel weights keeps every
  channel group's share of the gradient independent of the number of cells it
  spans; the calibration is worth 0.036--0.043 of R@1 and makes a 303M-parameter
  model train stably.
\end{itemize}

The training corpus is 311 animal rigs with 34 to 102 deformation joints across
108 distinct kinematic trees, carrying 77,894 captioned clips (6.2M frames) from
the AniMo4D annotation set~\cite{wang2025animo}. Scored by a skeleton-aware
text--motion evaluator, TopX reaches R@1 0.867 against 0.904 for real clips, with
FID 0.005, and retrieval and FID improve monotonically from 36M to 303M
parameters. The output is what a rig plays directly: per-joint rotations and a
world-space root trajectory, with no inverse-kinematics fit.

Rigs outside the library are served by the same forward pass. On four held-out
Truebones rigs (buffalo, gazelle, dragon and spider, the whole Truebones zoo being
kept out of training), a low-rank adapter trained on 8--26 clips brings the
FK--pose gap from 0.57--4.5 bone lengths to 0.23--0.35, with the largest drop on
the two rigs farthest from the library; one adapter pooled over eight flying rigs
removes the jitter excess on all seven scored rigs.

The paper is in submission to ICLR 2027~\cite{sun2026topx}.

\subsection{Collaborative work}

Four further motion systems were developed with colleagues in the group, each
building on components contributed here.

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{MotionDuet}~\cite{zhang2026motionduet}, published at CVPR 2026,
  aligns motion generation with the distribution of video-derived
  representations. Video cues from a pretrained model ground low-level dynamics
  while text supplies semantic intent; DUET fuses video-informed cues into the
  motion latent space, a DASH loss aligns generated trajectories with the
  distributional and structural statistics of video features, and an
  auto-guidance mechanism balances the two conditions using a weakened copy of
  the model. My contribution was to the dual-conditioning design and the
  training and evaluation of the fused model.

\item \textbf{MoGeFlow}~\cite{fang2026mogeflow} shows that PartVQ's
  group-specific codes carry measurable, non-random and decoder-causal
  kinematic geometry, and generates by learning a text-conditioned continuous
  flow over code embeddings rather than predicting categorical indices. It sets
  state of the art in R-Precision on HumanML3D and KIT-ML. My contribution was
  the PartVQ substrate it is built on and the codebook-geometry diagnostics.

\item \textbf{AnchorRoute}~\cite{fang2026anchorroute} uses sparse anchors as a
  single scaffold for both generation and refinement: anchors are injected into
  a frozen masked diffusion prior as condition memory, and the same anchors are
  then read as residuals whose timestamps define refinement intervals. My
  contribution was the attention-side injection mechanism carried over from
  KV-Control.

\item \textbf{UMO}~\cite{zhan2026umo} addresses unified sparse motion modelling
  for real-time co-speech avatars, to which I contributed on the sparse motion
  representation.
\end{itemize}

\section{Out-of-distribution specialisation for topology-agnostic generation}

\workitem{%
TopX makes the skeleton an input, and already serves unseen rigs zero-shot and
with a per-rig adapter. What it does not yet do is serve a new body plan with no
clips: bodies far from the library still need an adapter, and the per-cell
statistics every rig requires are computed from that rig's own motion. This work
closes that gap and completes Chapter~6.}{%
To serve a rig whose body plan is absent from training with no clips at all, and
to reduce the clips needed to reach in-library quality on the hardest bodies.}{%
The TopX results already point to where the difficulty sits. Zero-shot error is
small for rigs close to the library and large for the two farthest (4.5 bone
lengths for the dragon against 0.57 for the gazelle), and a single adapter pooled
over eight flying rigs removes the jitter excess on all of them. Out-of-distribution
difficulty is therefore organised by body plan rather than by individual rig.
Two consequences follow. First, an adapter shared across a body-plan family
transfers to members of that family never seen by the adapter, which replaces
per-rig adaptation with per-family adaptation. Second, a rig's per-cell
statistics are determined largely by its kinematic tree and rest pose, so they
can be predicted from the skeleton, which removes the last requirement for motion
data at deployment.}{%
\begin{enumerate}
\item Group the held-out Truebones rigs by body plan and report quality as a
  function of distance from the training library rather than as one aggregate.
\item Train a statistics predictor from kinematic tree and rest pose to per-cell
  mean and standard deviation, and evaluate true zero-clip deployment against
  the current zero arm, which uses statistics computed from real clips.
\item Train body-plan family adapters and test them on family members held out
  from the adapter, measuring the clips--quality curve against per-rig LoRA.
\item Compare against the Truebones-only reference and against
  AnyTop~\cite{gat2025anytop} and the concurrent UniMate on the same rigs.
\end{enumerate}}

\section{Multi-subtask motion generation}

\workitem{%
Every model in the thesis executes a single instruction. This work extends the
conditioning interface of Chapter~3 to ordered, composite instructions, and is
the last piece required for the thesis claim of control that is both precise and
general.}{%
To generate long-horizon motion from an ordered set of subtasks such that
transitions are generated rather than stitched, style and identity persist
across subtask boundaries, and the per-part control of Chapter~5 remains
available within any individual subtask.}{%
Existing approaches treat composition as a post-hoc problem: clips are generated
independently and blended~\cite{shafir2024priormdm,barquero2024flowmdm}, so the
transition is a smoothing artefact rather than something the model has learned.
Writing subtask boundaries into the sequence the model decodes makes the
transition part of the modelled distribution, improving both boundary quality
and global style consistency over blending. The part-tokenized substrate of
Chapter~5 is what makes this practical: tokens are already addressable per part
and per frame, so a boundary can be expressed without leaving the
representation, and MOGO's causal decoder already accepts a condition change
mid-sequence.}{%
\begin{enumerate}
\item Fix the subtask representation: how a boundary is encoded, and whether
  ordering is supplied as a hard schedule or inferred.
  \needsinput{the current design, and which of the two the model assumes}
\item Assemble training data with subtask annotations, from compositional
  corpora~\cite{lee2023multiact,zhang2023finemogen} or by segmenting long
  sequences. \needsinput{the dataset in use}
\item Train the compositional decoder on the part-tokenized backbone and verify
  that per-part controllability survives composition.
\item Evaluate transition quality, long-horizon drift and style consistency
  against blending-based baselines, and confirm that KV-Control injection still
  applies within a subtask.
\end{enumerate}}
